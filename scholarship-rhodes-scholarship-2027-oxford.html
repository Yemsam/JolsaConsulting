What is the Rhodes Scholarship?

Established in 1903, the Rhodes Scholarship is the world's oldest and most prestigious international postgraduate award. It enables exceptional young people from around the world to undertake full-time postgraduate study at the University of Oxford. The scholarship is merit-based and seeks to develop public-spirited leaders committed to making a positive difference in the world.

Administered by the Rhodes Trust in Oxford, the program awards over 100 fully funded scholarships each year to students from designated constituencies (geographic regions) around the world. Rhodes Scholars become part of a global community of leaders across every field imaginable, including heads of state, Nobel laureates, Supreme Court judges, entrepreneurs, and activists.

What does it cover?

  • Full tuition and university fees for the duration of your course at Oxford
  • Annual living stipend (currently approximately £19,092 per year, adjusted annually)
  • Economy return airfare to Oxford at the start and end of your scholarship
  • Health insurance coverage through the UK's National Health Service
  • Visa application costs and immigration health surcharge
  • Research and academic-related expenses
  • Duration: typically 2–3 years depending on your Oxford degree program; extensions possible in some cases
  • Support from the Rhodes Trust team and access to the global Rhodes Scholar network

Who is eligible?

  • Eligibility varies by Rhodes constituency (geographic region). You must apply through the constituency where you have citizenship or a sufficient connection.
  • General age requirement: Most constituencies require applicants to be between 18 and 27 years old on October 1, 2026 (i.e., born after October 1, 1999), though some constituencies have different age limits.
  • Must have completed (or be on track to complete) an undergraduate degree by the time you begin at Oxford in October 2027, with academic performance that meets or exceeds Oxford's specific entry requirements for your chosen course
  • Must demonstrate excellence across four key criteria: academic and intellectual distinction; energy to make a difference; moral character and integrity; and leadership potential
  • Must demonstrate commitment to others and to the common good through community engagement, service, or activism
  • No specific nationality restrictions—Rhodes Scholarships are available from most countries and regions globally, organized into constituencies

Application timeline

Note: Deadlines vary significantly by constituency. The dates below are illustrative examples. Always check the specific deadline for your constituency on the Rhodes Trust website.

  • June 1, 2026: Applications open for most constituencies worldwide
  • July 1, 2026: U.S. applications open
  • August 27, 2026 (23:59 EAT): Deadline for East Africa constituency (Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, South Sudan, Burundi)
  • September 24, 2026 (23:59 PT): Deadline for Canada
  • October 7, 2026 (23:59 ET): Deadline for United States
  • Varies, typically September–October 2026: Deadlines for other constituencies (Kenya, West Africa, Southern Africa, Global, India, Australia, etc.)
  • October–December 2026: Shortlisting, interviews, and final selections by constituency
  • October 2027: Scholars commence studies at Oxford

How to apply

  1. Determine your constituency. Visit the Rhodes Trust website and use the constituency finder to identify which Rhodes constituency you should apply through based on your citizenship and residency. Each constituency has specific eligibility requirements and deadlines.
  2. Check constituency-specific requirements. Some constituencies (e.g., U.S., Canada) require institutional endorsement from your university. Others allow direct applications. Read your constituency's application guidelines carefully.
  3. Choose your Oxford course. Research Oxford postgraduate programs that align with your academic interests and career goals. You don't need an Oxford offer to apply for the Rhodes, but you should have a clear plan for what you want to study.
  4. Prepare your application materials. This typically includes: a personal statement explaining your academic interests, leadership, service, and career aspirations; an academic CV; official transcripts; 4–8 letters of recommendation (number varies by constituency); and proof of academic qualifications.
  5. Submit your application through the Rhodes online application portal by your constituency's deadline. Some constituencies also require a certification form from your university.
  6. Prepare for interviews. Shortlisted candidates will be invited to in-person interviews in their constituencies, usually in October, November, or December 2026. Interviews assess all four selection criteria.
  7. If selected, apply to Oxford. Rhodes Scholars apply to Oxford after selection, usually in early 2027, with support from the Rhodes Trust. Selection as a Rhodes Scholar does not guarantee Oxford admission, but the Trust provides guidance and your Rhodes application materials can support your Oxford application.

Application tips

  • The Rhodes is looking for the whole person—academic brilliance alone is not enough. Demonstrate leadership, service to others, moral character, and a clear vision for positive impact.
  • In your personal statement, tell a coherent story: connect your past experiences, your Oxford study plans, and your future goals into a narrative of purpose-driven leadership.
  • Choose recommenders who know you well and can speak to different facets of your character—academics, community leaders, employers, mentors. Generic letters won't stand out.
  • Be authentic. The selection panels are experienced at spotting applicants who exaggerate or present a false persona. Show who you genuinely are and what you genuinely care about.
  • Research is critical: understand the history and values of the Rhodes Scholarship, know your constituency's selection process, and be prepared to articulate why Oxford and why now.
  • If your constituency requires institutional endorsement, engage with your university's fellowships office early—campus deadlines are often weeks before the Rhodes deadline.

FAQs

Can Nigerians apply for the Rhodes Scholarship?
Yes. Nigerian citizens may apply through the Rhodes Scholarship for West Africa constituency, which covers multiple West African countries. Check the Rhodes Trust website for the West Africa application timeline and specific requirements.

What courses can I study at Oxford as a Rhodes Scholar?
Rhodes Scholars can pursue any full-time postgraduate degree at Oxford, including taught Master's degrees (e.g., MSc, MPhil), research Master's degrees, second bachelor's degrees in certain subjects, or DPhil (PhD) programs.

Can I defer my Rhodes Scholarship?
No. The Rhodes Scholarship cannot be deferred to a later year. If you are selected for the 2027 cohort, you must begin at Oxford in October 2027 or forfeit the scholarship.

How competitive is the Rhodes Scholarship?
Extremely competitive. The overall acceptance rate is typically below 1%, though it varies by constituency. Strong academic performance is a baseline; the scholarship ultimately goes to applicants who demonstrate exceptional leadership, character, and commitment to service.

Always verify current constituency deadlines, eligibility criteria, and application procedures on the official Rhodes Trust website: Rhodes Scholarship. Select your constituency to see region-specific information.
