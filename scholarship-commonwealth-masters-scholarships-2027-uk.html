What are Commonwealth Master's Scholarships?

Commonwealth Master's Scholarships are prestigious awards funded by the UK government to support talented individuals from low- and middle-income Commonwealth countries to undertake full-time taught Master's study at UK universities. The program aims to support people who could not otherwise afford to study in the UK and who are committed to making a positive impact in their home countries.

Administered by the Commonwealth Scholarship Commission (CSC), the scholarships strengthen ties among Commonwealth nations and contribute to the sustainable development goals of partner countries. The CSC offers around 800 awards each year across various scholarship schemes.

What does it cover?

  • Full tuition fees paid directly to the UK university
  • Monthly living allowance (stipend): £1,378 per month, or £1,690 per month for study in London
  • Economy return airfare from your home country to the UK
  • Arrival allowance to help with initial costs
  • Thesis grant (if applicable)
  • Study travel grant for academic-related travel within the UK or overseas
  • Family allowances for widows, widowers, or single parents bringing one child under 16
  • Full scholarship duration: one academic year for taught Master's programs

Who is eligible?

  • Must be a citizen of or have been granted refugee status by an eligible low- or middle-income Commonwealth country (includes Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, Uganda, Tanzania, Pakistan, Bangladesh, India, and many others—check the official CSC country list)
  • Must be permanently resident in an eligible Commonwealth country
  • Must hold a first degree of at least upper second class (2:1) honours standard, or a lower second class degree plus a relevant postgraduate qualification (usually a Master's degree)
  • Must apply to study a one-year taught Master's program at a UK university aligned with your home country's development priorities
  • Must demonstrate commitment to development and how your studies will contribute to development in your home country
  • Cannot be currently studying or working in a high-income country
  • Must apply through your country's nominating agency (not directly to the CSC)

Application timeline

  • September 8, 2026: CSC Central application system opens
  • October 20, 2026, 16:00 BST: CSC application deadline
  • Note: Many nominating agencies in applicants' home countries set their own earlier deadlines—check with your nominating agency immediately
  • Late 2026 / Early 2027: Shortlisting and interviews (if required)
  • April/May 2027: Final selection results announced
  • September/October 2027: Studies commence in the UK

How to apply

  1. Identify your nominating agency. Commonwealth Scholarships require nomination by an agency in your home country (often a government ministry, national scholarship body, or British Council office). Visit the CSC website to find your country's nominating agency and their specific deadline, which may be earlier than October 20.
  2. Choose up to three eligible UK Master's courses. Select one-year taught Master's programs that align with your home country's development priorities and your career goals. You will rank these in order of preference on your application.
  3. Apply for admission to your chosen UK universities. You must apply separately to each university through their standard admissions process. Admission is not guaranteed by a CSC scholarship offer, and you need to demonstrate you meet each university's entry requirements.
  4. Complete the CSC Central online application. Register on the CSC Central portal at portal.csccentralonline.org.uk when it opens on September 8, 2026. Upload all required documents: academic transcripts, degree certificates, references (usually two), a development impact statement, and proof of citizenship.
  5. Submit to your nominating agency. Follow your nominating agency's procedures—they will review applications and forward a shortlist to the CSC. Ensure you meet your agency's internal deadline.
  6. Track your application through CSC Central and respond promptly if selected for an interview.

Application tips

  • Start early—the process involves three parallel applications: CSC scholarship, nominating agency nomination, and university admissions
  • Research your home country's development priorities and align your chosen courses and personal statement with them
  • In your development impact statement, be concrete: explain the specific problem you want to address, how your UK Master's will equip you, and what you will do when you return home
  • Choose universities and courses strategically; prestige matters less than fit with your development goals and likelihood of admission
  • Request strong references from people who know your academic work and development commitment (e.g., professors, employers, community leaders)
  • Commonwealth scholarships are about development impact, not just academic merit—demonstrate your commitment to returning home and making a difference

FAQs

Can Nigerian students apply?
Yes. Nigeria is an eligible country for Commonwealth Master's Scholarships. Nigerian applicants must apply through the Federal Scholarship Board or the designated nominating agency in Nigeria.

What if I don't have a 2:1 degree?
Applicants with a lower second class degree (2:2) may still be eligible if they also hold a relevant postgraduate qualification, usually a Master's degree with strong performance.

Can I apply directly to the CSC?
No. You must apply through your home country's nominating agency. Direct applications to the CSC are not accepted.

What are development priorities?
Each Commonwealth country has identified priority sectors (e.g., health, agriculture, education, governance). Your chosen course should align with these—check the CSC website for your country's profile.

Always verify current eligibility, deadlines, and application procedures on the official Commonwealth Scholarship Commission website: CSC Commonwealth Master's Scholarships. Contact your country's nominating agency for country-specific guidance.
